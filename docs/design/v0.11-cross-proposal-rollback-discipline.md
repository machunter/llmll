## 1. Motivation

LT-INV's §8 empirical-validation gate is the only v0.11 proposal with an *external* rollback mechanism. The gate is run on [`experiments/minimal-agent/001-two-agent-auth`](../../experiments/minimal-agent/) plus post-DL-B follow-up batches; the pass criteria are stated in LT-INV §8 (revised per the LT-INV review Gap #4 to add boundary-form-usage and no-material-regression conditions). Two rollback paths are pre-committed in the upstream professor direction memo at [`core-shell-inversion-direction.md §8.3`](core-shell-inversion-direction.md):

> **Path 1 — demote to opt-in.** Keep the grammar change available behind a per-module pragma or `--grammar=core-inversion` flag; default remains the v0.10 mixed regime. Grammar work preserved; polarity claim retracted until a future cycle.
>
> **Path 2 — retract the grammar change; ship LT-CDP + LT-PPR only.** Contract discriminative power and predicate-carrying `?proof-required` are valuable independently of the inversion; both can ship without LT-INV.

The professor direction memo specified the *LT-INV* consequences of each path. It did not specify what LT-CDP, LT-PPR, LT-INT, and REF-META-1 ship as under those paths — and that under-specification is the C-2 finding the REF-META-1 review surfaced.

This artifact closes that gap. Each downstream proposal's shipping conditions are specified per gate outcome. The settlement is a coordination artifact, not a design proposal: it adjudicates the cross-proposal coupling under the rollback envelope rather than introducing new semantics.

The C-2 finding's load-bearing claim:

> Paths (1) and (2) require explicit specification of LT-CDP and LT-PPR's shipping conditions, not just LT-INV's. Without this, the rollback decision is on a moving target.

LT-INT (INT-2 codegen switch) is gate-independent — INT-PRE adjudicated `int-2-clear` at commit `8cac520` and the LT-INT engineer build proceeds regardless of the LT-INV gate outcome. REF-META-1 is also gate-independent — it is pure spec pedagogy and the doc-lead promotion to `LLMLL.md §3.4 / §5` proceeds regardless. The artifact covers them briefly for completeness.

## 2. Outcomes under the LT-INV §8 gate result

Three outcomes are pre-committed: gate passes (Outcome 0); gate fails and rolls back to LT-INV opt-in-only (Outcome 1); gate fails and LT-INV is retracted entirely (Outcome 2).

### 2.1 Outcome 0 — gate passes

**LT-INV:** default grammar flips per LT-INV §8.4 step (4). `def` becomes the canonical strict-core form; `def-shell` is the permissive marked form. JSON-AST `schemaVersion` bumps `0.5.0 → 0.6.0` (additive enumeration at the statement-kind level). Mechanical migration runs across [`examples/`](../../examples/) per LT-INV §6. The `--grammar=core-inversion` opt-in flag is retired in a subsequent release.

**LT-CDP:** ships as proposed. `discriminative_axis` reported for every `def`-form function per LT-CDP §5. `def-shell` functions get no DP reported (per LT-CDP §2 out-of-scope). The `(spec-entropy ...)` annotation is admitted on both `def` and `def-shell` per LT-CDP §3.

**LT-PPR:** ships as proposed. Predicate-carrying form admitted in `def-shell`; rejected in `def` with a *core-membership-violation* diagnostic per LT-PPR §6.2 (inheriting LT-INV §1.4 verbatim). Trust label stays `asserted` per LT-PPR §6.1. Runtime-assertion fallback emitted at codegen with `runtime_check_emitted: true` by default.

**LT-INT:** ships per `int-2-boundary-shims.md` Rev 3 (commit `90b759e`); gate-independent of LT-INV. `int` lowers to `Integer` at codegen; verifier semantics unchanged.

**REF-META-1:** ships per the Rev 2 proposal (pending post-this-artifact); gate-independent of LT-INV. Doc-lead promotion to `LLMLL.md §3.4 / §5` proceeds.

**Schema deltas:** JSON-AST `schemaVersion 0.5.0 → 0.6.0` (LT-INV + LT-PPR bundle); `trust_report_version 1.1.0 → 1.2.0` (LT-CDP). LT-PPR rides LT-INV's `schemaVersion` bump (additive predicate field on `hole-proof-required`); standalone LT-PPR ships as additive on v0.5.0 with no bump per its §7 footnote.

**Example migration:** all twelve example directories migrated mechanically per LT-INV §6. The boundary-form usage distribution measured during the gate determines whether `def`-default or `def-shell`-default predominates in the migrated corpus.

### 2.2 Outcome 1 — gate fails; rollback to LT-INV opt-in-only

**LT-INV:** grammar change shipped but *not* default. `def` and `def-shell` keywords are admitted only when the `--grammar=core-inversion` opt-in flag is set. Outside the flag, the default grammar is the v0.10 `def-logic` regime. JSON-AST `schemaVersion` bumps `0.5.0 → 0.6.0` to admit the new statement kinds (so flag users can parse `def` / `def-shell` documents), but the example migration is skipped — examples stay on `def-logic`.

**LT-CDP:** `discriminative_axis` is reported only under the `--grammar=core-inversion` opt-in flag — i.e., only when the function is parsed as `def` (the core form). Default-grammar `def-logic` functions get *no* CDP reporting. The rationale: LT-CDP's trivial-body enumeration assumes the function body is in the body-faithful set, which the `def` grammar guarantees syntactically; `def-logic` bodies have no such guarantee, and CDP measurement on shell-form-equivalent bodies falls into LT-CDP edge case #4 (`undefined-due-to-enumeration-limit`) for most non-trivial constructs.

The `(spec-entropy ...)` annotation is parser-accepted on `def-logic` functions but does not raise diagnostics (since no CDP is computed to compare against the annotation). On `def` / `def-shell` under the opt-in flag, the annotation behaves per LT-CDP §3 (raise on `:strict` low-DP, suppress on `:intentional` / `:unknown`).

**LT-PPR:** predicate-carrying form admitted in `def-shell` *under the opt-in flag*; rejected in `def` per LT-PPR §6.2 (under flag). **Outside the opt-in flag, the predicate-carrying form is *also rejected* in `def-logic`.** This is the load-bearing rule under Outcome 1: consumers do not get the predicate-carrying feature as a default — they must explicitly opt into LT-INV's grammar to get any predicate-carrying form. The rationale: LT-PPR §6.2's `def`-forbiddance is the asserted-tier-escape-hatch protection; admitting the predicate-carrying form in `def-logic` (default) would re-introduce the escape hatch in the v0.10 surface that the inversion is precisely designed to prevent. Better to gate the entire feature on the opt-in flag than to admit it without the protection.

Trust label stays `asserted` per LT-PPR §6.1 in either case (under flag or rejected; the rejection is parse-time, so no trust-label question arises for rejected programs).

**LT-INT:** ships per `int-2-boundary-shims.md`; gate-independent. Same as Outcome 0.

**REF-META-1:** ships per the Rev 2 proposal; gate-independent. Same as Outcome 0.

**Schema deltas:** JSON-AST `schemaVersion 0.5.0 → 0.6.0` (admits the new statement kinds for flag users); `trust_report_version` bump is contingent on LT-CDP's `discriminative_axis` being a top-level field. Under Outcome 1, CDP is reported only under flag — the `discriminative_axis` block is optional on the per-function trust-entry shape. Recommend: bump `trust_report_version 1.1.0 → 1.2.0` regardless (additive optional field); downstream consumers ignoring the optional field continue to work.

**Example migration:** *skipped*. Examples stay on `def-logic`. The migration tooling (LT-INV §6) is shipped but not invoked on the canonical corpus; users can invoke it locally with `--grammar=core-inversion` if they wish.

### 2.3 Outcome 2 — gate fails; retract LT-INV entirely

**LT-INV:** retracted. `def` and `def-shell` keywords are not introduced. No JSON-AST `schemaVersion` bump from LT-INV (LT-CDP and LT-PPR ship their schema deltas independently per below). The grammar work — whitelist production, callee-restriction predicate, hole-admission asymmetry — is preserved in `core-shell-inversion-proposal.md` (status flipped to `Retracted (Rev 1) — empirical gate failed on YYYY-MM-DD; preserved for future cycle`) but does not ship in v0.11.

**LT-CDP:** ships against `def-logic` with the body-faithful set as the implicit scope. The trivial-body enumeration is applied to every `def-logic` function; the score is *defined* when the function's contracted-clause obligations are within the body-faithful set per `LLMLL.md §5.3.5`, and *undefined-due-to-enumeration-limit* (LT-CDP edge case #4) otherwise. The `discriminative_axis.warnings` field reports the enumeration-limit case explicitly so downstream consumers distinguish "spec is weak" from "metric did not apply." The `(spec-entropy ...)` annotation is admitted on `def-logic` and behaves per LT-CDP §3.

JSON-AST `schemaVersion` bump for the `(spec-entropy ...)` annotation: under Outcome 2, LT-CDP ships an independent bump `0.5.0 → 0.5.1` (additive optional annotation, additive-only major-minor preserved). The bump is small and discharges the annotation; no v0.6.0 bundle.

**LT-PPR:** ships *without* the `def`-forbiddance. Predicate-carrying form is admissible in `def-logic`. **This is a contingent acceptance**: under Outcome 2, the asserted-tier escape hatch re-enters the default form, and LT-PPR §6.2's adjudication is *contingently undone* by the absence of LT-INV's grammar. The proposal's §6.2 rationale (the polarity inversion is undermined by admitting `?proof-required` in `def`) is moot because there is no polarity inversion under Outcome 2.

The runtime-assertion fallback ships unchanged (per LT-PPR §4.2); the trust-report fields `predicate_form` / `predicate_text` / `runtime_check_emitted` ship unchanged (per LT-PPR §5). The semantic content of LT-PPR — making the predicate machine-readable, emitting runtime-assertion — is preserved; only the `def`-forbiddance is dropped.

JSON-AST `schemaVersion` bump for the `predicate` field on `hole-proof-required`: under Outcome 2, LT-PPR ships an independent bump `0.5.0 → 0.5.1` (additive optional field). If both LT-CDP and LT-PPR bump independently under Outcome 2, the bumps are coordinated to a single `0.5.1` rather than `0.5.1 → 0.5.2`.

**LT-INT:** ships per `int-2-boundary-shims.md`; gate-independent. Same as Outcomes 0 and 1.

**REF-META-1:** ships per the Rev 2 proposal; gate-independent. Same as Outcomes 0 and 1.

**Example migration:** not applicable. Examples stay on `def-logic`.

## 3. Sequencing rule

The cross-proposal coupling described above affects the *schema-bump* and *example-migration* steps of the v0.11 engineer build, not the *per-proposal feature implementation* steps. The roadmap §8.4 sequencing is correct at the implementation level; the C-2 settlement adds gate-conditional discipline at the integration level.

**Implementation-level work is gate-independent:**

- LT-INV's grammar production (whitelist + callee-restriction predicate + hole-admission asymmetry) is implemented as a flag-gated parser path. The implementation can proceed independent of the gate result; only the flag default (Outcome 0) vs flag-only (Outcome 1) vs not-shipped (Outcome 2) is gate-conditional.
- LT-CDP's CDP measurement pipeline (`WeaknessCheck.hs` extension, equivalence-class counting, score normalization, trust-report emit) is implemented unconditionally. Only the *scope* of reporting (`def`-only / opt-in-only / `def-logic`-all) is gate-conditional, and the scope is a configuration parameter, not a code path.
- LT-PPR's `HoleKind.HProofRequired` extension (parser, typechecker, codegen, trust-report) is implemented unconditionally. Only the grammar-admission discipline (`def`-forbiddance) is gate-conditional, and it is implemented as a *parser predicate* gated by the surrounding-form context. Under Outcome 2, the predicate degrades to `true` and the predicate-carrying form is admitted in `def-logic`.
- LT-INT and REF-META-1 are gate-independent at every level.

**Integration-level work is gate-conditional:**

- JSON-AST `schemaVersion` bumps: Outcome 0 / Outcome 1 → `0.5.0 → 0.6.0` (LT-INV + LT-PPR bundle); Outcome 2 → `0.5.0 → 0.5.1` (LT-CDP + LT-PPR independent additives, coordinated).
- `trust_report_version` bump: same `1.1.0 → 1.2.0` (additive) under all three outcomes; `discriminative_axis` is an optional field whose population is gate-conditional but whose schema definition is uniform.
- Example migration: only under Outcome 0. Skipped under Outcomes 1 and 2.
- Roadmap row updates: doc-lead bumps the v0.11 milestone rows on the relevant outcome (Shipped, Shipped-opt-in-only, or Retracted).

**Engineer parallel-work compatibility:**

The roadmap §8.4 sequencing step (2) — "compiler-engineer ships the LT-INV grammar change behind an explicit opt-in flag; LT-CDP and LT-PPR ship in parallel" — is consistent with this settlement. LT-CDP and LT-PPR engineer work proceeds with the unconditional implementation; the gate-conditional integration steps run after the gate measurement.

Under Outcome 2, the LT-PPR implementation's `def`-forbiddance predicate is degraded to `true`. This is the only LT-PPR work that is partially reverted. LT-CDP's scope parameter is set to "all `def-logic` functions" under Outcome 2; this is configuration, not revert.

The implementation cost under each outcome:

- Outcome 0: full implementation as specified; mechanical migration; schema bump. No revert.
- Outcome 1: full implementation; flag-only default; no example migration. The flag's *default-off* is configuration; no implementation cost beyond Outcome 0's flag-gating.
- Outcome 2: full LT-CDP and LT-PPR implementation; LT-PPR's `def`-forbiddance predicate degraded; LT-INV implementation preserved on a feature branch but not merged to default. Compiler-team cost: small additional configuration step at integration, plus the merge-vs-branch decision.

## 4. Diagnostic-text ergonomics deferral (C-3)

The REF-META-1 review's cross-proposal observation C-3 flagged that no v0.11 proposal owns the *agent-actionable diagnostic-text surface* for parse-rejected `def` bodies. Under Outcome 0, the diagnostic-text question is load-bearing: every parse rejection in `def` (per LT-INV §3.2 whitelist) needs an agent-actionable error message. The proposal sketches the form but does not specify the catalog of diagnostics.

Under this settlement:

**C-3 defer:** the diagnostic-text design is v0.12+ scope. The v0.11 engineer build will produce *correct* diagnostics (the grammar-production rejection is well-formed); the *agent-actionable enhancement* (cite the verification matrix row, propose the migration, distinguish parse-time from typecheck-time rejection) is deferred. The natural home is a follow-up language-team proposal (REF-META-2 candidate, or a dedicated diagnostic-ergonomics proposal).

The deferral is justified because:

- Diagnostic-text quality is an *ergonomic* concern, not a *correctness* concern. Outcome 0 ships correct diagnostics; Outcomes 1 and 2 ship fewer diagnostics (less of the grammar is rejected).
- Under Outcomes 1 and 2, the diagnostic-text question is less load-bearing — the default grammar admits the constructs that under Outcome 0 would be rejected, so the rejection-diagnostic surface is smaller.
- Empirical data from the LT-INV §8 gate's measurement of agent-loop iteration count will inform what diagnostic-text shape best supports agent repair flows; specifying the catalog before the empirical data is premature.

**Recommended follow-up:** if Outcome 0 lands, schedule a v0.12 language-team proposal addressing diagnostic-text ergonomics for the new core-grammar rejections, using the §8 measurement data as the empirical baseline.

## 8. Risks and open questions

1. **Outcome-2 schema-bump coordination requires engineer-side discipline.** *Classify: spec-drift.* Source: §3 names LT-CDP and LT-PPR coordinating to a single `0.5.1` bump under Outcome 2 rather than two independent bumps. If the engineer ships one before the other and bumps independently, the resulting state is `0.5.1` (after LT-CDP) → `0.5.2` (after LT-PPR), which works but is uglier than `0.5.0 → 0.5.1` (joint). Bite: low. **Mitigation:** engineer hand-off summarizes the joint-bump expectation; doc-lead's release-process check verifies the schema-bump cardinality matches the proposal-bundle cardinality.

2. **LT-PPR's contingent acceptance under Outcome 2 contradicts its §6.2 adjudication as written.** *Classify: scope.* Source: §2.3 above. LT-PPR §6.2's rationale (asserted-tier escape hatch protection) is moot under Outcome 2 because there is no polarity inversion to protect. The proposal's §6.2 should acknowledge this contingency in Rev 2 — not retract the adjudication, but state it explicitly as "settled under Outcome 0; relaxed under Outcome 2 per the C-2 settlement at [`v0.11-cross-proposal-rollback-discipline.md`](v0.11-cross-proposal-rollback-discipline.md)." Bite: low. **Mitigation:** LT-PPR Rev 2's §-new contingent-shipping rule names this.

3. **Outcome 1's rejection-of-predicate-carrying-form-outside-flag may surprise consumers.** *Classify: verification-ergonomics.* Source: §2.2 above. Under Outcome 1, consumers who do not set `--grammar=core-inversion` get *no* predicate-carrying `?proof-required` — the feature is gated entirely on the opt-in flag. The alternative (admit the predicate-carrying form in `def-logic` outside the flag) was considered and rejected because it would silently introduce the asserted-tier escape hatch the inversion was designed to prevent. Bite: low. The Outcome 1 path is itself a fallback; consumers reaching it accept the constrained behavior. **Mitigation:** the LT-PPR Rev 2 §-new contingent-shipping rule explicitly names the Outcome-1 behavior (rejected-outside-flag) so consumers can plan migration.

4. **The C-2 settlement does not address what happens if the gate result is ambiguous** (e.g., one axis improves, one regresses materially). *Classify: scope.* Source: LT-INV Rev 2 §8 pass criteria. The Rev 2 criteria (per the LT-INV review Gap #4) are conjunctive — *all three* sub-conditions must hold. An ambiguous result fails the conjunction and routes to Outcome 1 or Outcome 2 per the rollback path adjudication; this artifact does not duplicate that adjudication. Bite: low. **Mitigation:** LT-INV Rev 2 §8 names the routing.

5. **Outcome 0's example migration produces a corpus where the boundary-form distribution is the v0.11 baseline for future measurement.** *Classify: ergonomic.* Source: §2.1 above. If the migration produces, say, 80% `def` and 20% `def-shell`, that distribution becomes the reference for future gate-style measurements (e.g., a hypothetical v0.12 measurement comparing pre/post on some other dimension). The reference is fine; it is named here so future measurements know the baseline. Bite: low. No mitigation needed.
