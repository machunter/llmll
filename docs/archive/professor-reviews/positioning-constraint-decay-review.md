## Restatement

The language-team has proposed a three-part positioning move around Dente, Satriani, and Papotti (arXiv 2605.06445, May 2026): admit the paper as an *external concept anchor* for the constraint-decay failure mode (not as solution validation), repair a four-claim mapping (refinement types / capability imports / hole-driven dev / stratified verification ↔ paper-measured failure modes) before any external release, and defer the Conduit benchmark on design-discipline rather than bandwidth grounds. Two questions accompany the proposal: whether the verified-language design-reference set has published agent-coding evaluations under analogous stacked-constraint conditions, and whether "constraint decay" has a prior name in the PL evaluation literature. This review assesses both the questions and the broader proposal.

The arXiv ID format (YYMM.NNNNN → year 2026, month 05, paper 6445) is well-formed, and Papotti is a real researcher (EURECOM, data management and LLM-for-data tradition), so the citation is plausible on its face. ## Gaps and hazards

1. **Single-source dependency on a 3-week-old preprint.** Severity: **blocks the proposal as-stated for any external venue with a long horizon (paper draft, journal submission, recorded talk).** Classification: scope / soundness-of-external-claim. The LT identifies this as Risk 5 with severity "low" — this underweights it. A preprint that age has no replication, no peer review, no field consensus on instrumentation. If Dente et al. is later refuted, withdrawn, or shown to have instrumentation flaws, every external artefact that cites it as the primary anchor inherits the problem. Mitigation: cite Dente et al. in concert with at least two adjacent works, so the anchor is structural rather than single-source. The Sketch/SyGuS spec-complexity-scaling tradition (Solar-Lezama 2006, Alur et al. 2013) and the IFEval instruction-following-degradation work (Zhou et al. 2023) are the two natural co-citations: together they establish that *agent performance degrades monotonically as constraint complexity rises* is a recurring finding across three distinct research traditions, of which Dente et al. is the verified-domain instance.

2. **Internal-proxy claim rests on orthogonal axes.** Severity: **blocks the proposal's part-3 framing as-stated.** Classification: empirical / scope. The LT writes that `experiments/repair-loop/` "already measures pass-rate degradation as constraints stack." It does not. Per [`language-comparison-experiments.md §"Proposed Framework Shape"`](language-comparison-experiments.md), the harness varies *agent × problem × target language × attempt* on a *fixed constraint set*. Dente et al. vary the *constraint stack* on a *fixed contract* with *fixed framework × fixed agent*. These are perpendicular axes. The harness's Correctness/Assurance split is also perpendicular to L0→L3 — Correctness/Assurance measures *kind of evidence the same constraint set produces*, not *how constraints accumulate*. An alert verification reader will see the orthogonality immediately, and the proposal loses the trust it gains in part 1 by overclaiming in part 3. Recommended fix: either drop the internal-proxy claim, or reframe it precisely — "we have built measurement infrastructure for the language-axis variant of constraint-bearing agent authorship; the constraint-layering axis Dente et al. measure is unmeasured by us and would require a sibling harness variant."

3. **Stratified-verification ↔ pass@1 mapping is the wrong metric pairing, even though it is the strongest direction.** Severity: complicates Part 2. Classification: empirical / spec-drift. Pass@1 is a single-attempt success rate — natural for non-iterative code generation. LLMLL's three-channel obligation report (the construct the LT correctly identifies as the strongest mapping) is *designed for iteration*: an obligation-report-aware agent reads compile-time feedback and revises. The natural dependent variable is convergence-to-fixpoint under bounded iteration, or pass@k with k matching repair-loop budget — not pass@1. [`empirical-methodology.md §"The criterion conflates two distinct claims"`](../../experiments/methodology.md) settled exactly this point internally: the project explicitly endorses iterative measurement and demotes first-round-only measurement to "one regime among several." Citing pass@1 as the metric the obligation report addresses contradicts the project's own settled methodological commitment. Recommended re-framing: the obligation report converts unbounded-divergence agent behaviour into bounded-iteration convergence under a structured feedback signal. This is also the empirically harder claim to demonstrate — pass@k is not zero in Dente et al., it just degrades — so the LT should be ready for the harder question: *does the obligation report bound the iteration count better than free-form compile errors?*

4. **[`specification-sources.md §1`](specification-sources.md) connection is the proposal's strongest move and it is buried.** Severity: complicates positioning shape (recoverable). Classification: design-memory undersold. The Conduit benchmark fixes an OpenAPI contract and varies non-functional constraints around it. [`specification-sources.md §1`](specification-sources.md) names spec-from-external-standards as the *strongest near-term answer* to the spec-source bottleneck and lists OpenAPI / RFCs / NIST standards as the canonical instances. This is a tight, citable, defensible connection that ties the cited paper to a project-internal design commitment that has been settled for over a month. It is materially stronger than any of the four mapping claims because it speaks to the *input shape* of LLMLL's pipeline rather than the *solution mechanisms* — and input shape is a less contestable claim. The LT places this in Edge Case 4. It belongs in Part 1, at the top, as the primary alignment claim.

5. **The differentiation question is unasked.** Severity: complicates external register. Classification: scope. The LT correctly notes that Liquid Haskell + `persistent` + Servant has the surface today, but it does not ask the next question: *why is the verified-language ecosystem not already used for backend agent authorship at scale?* Per the literature located above, the answer is not "because the verification machinery is missing" — the machinery has existed for over a decade. The answer is "because the agent-loop integration is missing, and the existing surfaces were not designed for agent authorship." That is where LLMLL's positioning lives, and the LT proposal needs to state it explicitly. Otherwise the verified-language reader concludes "this is Liquid Haskell with worse libraries and a citation to a preprint."

6. **"Stratified verification" is project-internal vocabulary that will misread externally.** Severity: low; cosmetic but worth fixing before external release. Classification: vocabulary discipline. In the broader PL literature "stratified" refers to predicative type theory (Russell-style universe stratification), Martin-Löf's universe hierarchies, or stratified negation in logic programming. LLMLL's use — synonymous with the three-channel obligation report (type / contract / trust) — is local to the project. External PL readers will misparse. For external register, use "three-channel obligation report" or "tiered verification" (which is at least less overloaded). Reserve "stratified" for project-internal docs.

7. **The deferral framing in Part 3 is one-sided.** Severity: low. Classification: argumentation. Part 3 argues that running Conduit would constitute design-by-benchmark, which is correctly identified as a project-discipline failure. But the symmetric cost — *not* running the benchmark leaves the field's strongest empirical anchor for the failure mode unaddressed by LLMLL — is unstated. The honest framing is: this is a tradeoff between two costs, the project has chosen design-discipline over empirical-anchor-completeness, and the choice is recorded as a deliberate scope position. The LT's framing currently reads as though only one side has costs, which is fragile under external pushback ("why are you so confident this won't matter?").

## Recommendation

The proposal is structurally correct — the three-part shape (admit-as-anchor / repair-mapping / defer-with-discipline) is right, and the LT's instinct to demote claims 1–2 and foreground claims 3–4 is sound. The recommended revision is *structural promotion of the spec-source connection* and *structural demotion of the four-claim mapping*:

**Promote** *spec-from-external-standards ↔ Dente et al.'s fixed-contract premise* to the primary positioning move. This is the only mapping that ties the paper to a settled project design commitment (over a month old in `specification-sources.md`), survives the verified-language design-reference set's scrutiny (the LH/F\*/Dafny tradition is not differentiated on OpenAPI ingestion either, so the project is not making a comparative claim it cannot defend), and matches the paper's actual instrumentation (fixed contract, varied constraints). The framing: *Dente et al. validate the failure-mode side of the spec-from-RFC argument LLMLL has been making methodologically — agents fail at non-functional constraint compliance when the functional contract is fixed and known; LLMLL's bet is that an explicit, machine-verifiable contract surface plus agent-loop integration around it shifts the failure surface measurably.*

**Demote** the four-claim mapping to a secondary "what mechanisms might address this" section, with all four claims marked direction-of-fit, not capability-of-fit. The LT's tabulated repair (refinement-types → aspirational, capabilities → partial, holes → direction-of-fit, stratified-verification → strongest) is correct in its differential labelling; the move is to demote the whole table rather than relying on per-row labels to do the work. External readers parse table presence as endorsement; differential labels do not survive translation into slide decks.

**Drop or precisely reframe** the internal-proxy claim. The cleanest version: "Our existing harness measures a *related* but *perpendicular* degradation axis (language-axis Correctness/Assurance variation on fixed constraints). The constraint-layering axis Dente et al. measure is currently unmeasured under LLMLL and would require a sibling harness variant. This is recorded as a deferred experiment, not a current capability."

**Reframe** the pass@1 mapping. The obligation report's natural target is bounded-iteration convergence under structured feedback, not pass@1. State this; it aligns the external claim with the project's settled methodological commitment in `empirical-methodology.md`.

**Cite at least two adjacent works alongside Dente et al.** Sketch/SyGuS spec-complexity scaling and IFEval-style instruction-following degradation are natural co-citations. The structural argument becomes *agent performance degrades monotonically as constraint complexity rises is a recurring finding across three research traditions (synthesis, instruction-following, verified backend code); LLMLL targets the third*. This is more durable than a single-preprint anchor.

**Verify the paper before citing it.** The user (or doc-lead at promotion time) should pull arXiv 2605.06445 and confirm the title, authors, methodology, and headline numbers match the description above. This is mechanical and cheap; not doing it before external release is the avoidable failure mode.

**Direct answer to LT Question 1 (verified-language ecosystem agent-coding evaluations).** To my reading, the verified-language design-reference set (Liquid Haskell, F\*, Dafny, Idris) has **not** published agent-coding evaluations under analogous stacked-constraint conditions. The adjacent literature — LLM-assisted theorem proving (Polu/Sutskever 2020, Lample et al. 2022, Yang et al. 2023, Azerbayev et al. 2024) — addresses proof-search, not program authorship under refinement constraints. Non-verified-language agent-coding evaluations (Chen et al. 2021 HumanEval, Jimenez et al. 2024 SWE-bench) do not use refinement-type targets. The quadrant is empty; Dente et al. is uniquely positioned, which strengthens the *uniqueness* claim but weakens the *robustness* claim (no peer-reviewed counter-evaluation exists). Strategic implication: LLMLL is not entering a literature where it has to differentiate against a prior treatment; it is entering a literature whose only published instance is a preprint. Both the opportunity and the exposure are real.

**Direct answer to LT Question 2 ("constraint decay" prior name).** "Constraint decay" as Dente et al. operationalize it does not, to my reading, have a prior name in the PL evaluation literature. The closest adjacent constructs are spec-complexity scaling in the synthesis tradition (Sketch, SyGuS — measures synthesizer cost, not correctness), instruction-following degradation in the LLM-evaluation tradition (IFEval and successors — not formal-constraint domain), and tool/capability degradation in the agent-tool literature (Toolformer, ToolBench — not constraint-correctness specifically). The Dente et al. construct is at the intersection of these three, which is genuinely novel as instrumentation. Strategic implication for the LT's positioning: the citation should claim the *concept* is novel-as-named-and-measured-here, not claim it is a long-recognized PL problem that LLMLL addresses. Overclaiming the concept's heritage will not survive a careful reader; understating its novelty undersells the anchor.

## Open questions for the language-team

1. **Confirm the metric reframing for the obligation-report claim.** The current draft maps three-channel obligation reporting to "pass@1 zeroing" reduction. Per Hazard 3, the obligation report's natural metric is bounded-iteration convergence, not pass@1, and this aligns with the project's own settled methodological commitment in `empirical-methodology.md`. Will the revised positioning use bounded-iteration-convergence framing, or is there a reason to retain pass@1 (for instance, alignment with a particular external audience's metric vocabulary) that overrides the internal methodological coherence?

2. **Specify what the demoted four-claim mapping looks like in the revised proposal.** Per the Recommendation, the table should be demoted from primary to secondary, and the *spec-from-external-standards* connection promoted to primary. The LT proposal sketches this in Edge Case 4 but does not commit to the structural inversion. Confirm: in the next-revision draft, does the four-claim mapping live as a primary structural section (with differential labelling) or as a secondary "what mechanisms might address this" section under a primary spec-source positioning move? The two shapes signal very different things to an external reader, and the choice should be explicit.
