## 1. Motivation

LLMLL has been arguing methodologically — through [`strategic-positioning.md`](strategic-positioning.md), [`specification-sources.md`](specification-sources.md), and [`empirical-methodology.md`](../../experiments/methodology.md) — that the agent-coding failure surface is dominated by the spec-source problem and by constraint-compliance failure under stacked non-functional requirements. The argument has not, until now, been able to cite peer-style empirical material for the failure-mode side: the project has measured its own apparatus (`experiments/repair-loop/`, `experiments/minimal-agent/`) but has not had an external instrument to point at when stating *the failure exists in the form we say it does*.

Dente, Satriani, and Papotti (arXiv 2605.06445, May 2026) supply that anchor. They fix a single OpenAPI contract (RealWorld Conduit), layer non-functional constraints across L0 (functional spec only) → L3 (architecture + database + ORM), and measure two agent scaffolds × ten LLMs across five providers (Mistral, Qwen, MiniMax, Moonshot, OpenAI) × eight frameworks over ~5B tokens. Headline findings: capable configurations lose ~30 percentage points in assertion pass rate from L0 to L3 (best case −17pp, worst case −45.5pp); pass@1 collapses to single digits at L3 (the strongest L3 config reaches only 8.3% pass@1 despite 78.6% assertion pass rate); data-layer defects (incorrect query composition + ORM runtime violations) drive ~46% of agent logic failures (25.5% + 21.2%); convention-heavy frameworks (FastAPI, Django, Hono) trail minimal-explicit ones (Flask, Express, Koa) by 25–32 points under identical functional contracts. Their concluding call is for "structural awareness — through retrieval-augmented framework documentation, constraint-oriented planning, or targeted pre-training." None of the three is the LLMLL move; the paper does not consider putting the constraints in the language itself.

This proposal does *not* claim that LLMLL has empirically beaten Dente et al.'s measurement set. It claims that the *failure-mode side* of LLMLL's spec-source bet is now externally validated and can be cited as such, with disciplined separation between the validated half (failure mode exists, takes this shape) and the unvalidated half (LLMLL's mechanisms reduce it).

The structural move forced by the professor turn is an inversion of the natural temptation to lead with a four-claim mechanism mapping (refinement types ↔ data-layer / capability imports ↔ architecture / hole-driven dev ↔ framework idiosyncrasy / three-channel obligation report ↔ pass@1 zeroing). The mechanism mapping is the wrong primary move because none of the four mechanisms is a *differentiating verification capability* against the verified-language design-reference set — Liquid Haskell, F\*, Dafny, and Idris each have refinement types, capability-style enforcement, hole-driven elaboration, and tiered verification machinery, and have had them for years. The primary positioning claim must rest on what LLMLL is operationalizing that the verified-language ecosystem has not, and that is the *spec-from-external-standards-as-agent-pipeline-input* pathway.

## 2. Primary positioning move: Dente et al. validates the failure-mode side of the spec-from-external-standards bet

The Conduit benchmark fixes an OpenAPI contract and varies non-functional constraints around it. [`specification-sources.md §1`](specification-sources.md) — settled April 2026 — names spec-from-external-standards as *the strongest near-term answer* to the spec-source bottleneck, with OpenAPI, RFCs, NIST standards, and ERC standards as the canonical instances. The alignment is exact: Dente et al.'s methodology operationalizes precisely the spec-source pathway the project has already committed to as its strongest pitch, and measures what happens to translation-plus-compliance when the constraint stack grows.

Proposed external-positioning text for promotion into [`strategic-positioning.md`](strategic-positioning.md) and abbreviated for [`specification-sources.md §1`](specification-sources.md):

> Recent empirical work measures the failure surface LLMLL's design premise targets. Dente, Satriani, and Papotti (arXiv 2605.06445, 2026) show that capable agent-model configurations on a fixed OpenAPI contract lose ~30 percentage points in assertion pass rate as non-functional constraints layer from L0 (functional spec only) to L3 (full structural constraints), with data-layer defects driving ~46% of agent failures. The fixed-contract premise is exactly the spec-source pathway LLMLL's design has bet on as its strongest near-term answer (`specification-sources.md §1`, April 2026): when the contract surface is explicit, machine-readable, and external (OpenAPI, RFCs, NIST, ERC), the agent's task reduces to translation-plus-compliance rather than spec invention. Dente et al. measure what happens to translation-plus-compliance when the constraint stack grows; LLMLL's bet is that an explicit, machine-verifiable contract surface plus agent-loop integration around it shifts the failure curve measurably. This is the empirical anchor for the input-shape half of the pitch. The output-side claim — whether LLMLL's particular mechanisms reduce the measured failure — is an open empirical question we have not yet answered against their measurement set.

This is the strongest defensible alignment for three reasons. First, it ties the citation to a project commitment that pre-dates the paper by over a month — the spec-from-external-standards argument is not retrofitted to fit the citation. Second, it speaks to the input shape of LLMLL's pipeline rather than to solution mechanisms, which is a less contestable claim and one that survives close reading by the verified-language design-reference set. Third, none of LH, F\*, Dafny, or Idris has a published spec-from-OpenAPI agent pipeline either: LLMLL is not differentiating against existing infrastructure, it is naming a pathway none of the verified-language tradition has yet operationalized for agents.

## 3. Secondary: candidate mechanisms LLMLL brings to bear

The four-claim mechanism mapping that motivated the original conversation is preserved here as a *secondary* discussion under the primary move, with the explicit framing that **none of the four is a differentiating verification capability**. The verified-language design-reference set has had the mechanisms — refinement types, capability-style enforcement, hole-driven elaboration, tiered verification — for years. LLMLL differentiates on *agent-loop integration of an existing verification surface*, not on the verification surface itself. The mechanisms below are how LLMLL composes that surface into an agent loop; they are not new verification primitives.

| Candidate mechanism | Status against current LLMLL surface | External register |
|---|---|---|
| Refinement predicates → constraint-shaped contract slots | **Direction-of-fit.** Scalar-shaped predicates over in-memory data are built ([`compiler/src/LLMLL/FixpointEmit.hs`](../../compiler/src/LLMLL/FixpointEmit.hs)); the SQL/ORM surface Dente et al.'s data-layer category measures is absent from LLMLL. | "A future direction; current refinement scope covers scalar invariants, not the SQL/ORM surface measured here." |
| Capability-typed effect rows → architecture-violation surface | **Partial.** CAP-1 catches reaches for undeclared IO capabilities at compile time ([`LLMLL.md §13`](../../LLMLL.md)). Project-architecture-convention violations (controller calls DB directly) are not enforced and would require a module-graph policy not currently specified. | "Compile-time error at the IO-effect seam; not at the controller/service/repository seam." |
| Typed holes with structured obligation reports → framework-idiosyncrasy slots | **Direction-of-fit at small scale.** OBLIG-0/1/2/3 shipped v0.10; demonstrated on banking-ledger / TOTP / hangman scale. Framework-conformance scale is unmeasured. | "Replaces guess-the-framework-idiom with constrained-search inside a contract-shaped slot; framework-scale evidence pending." |
| Three-channel obligation report (type/contract/trust) → bounded-iteration convergence under structured feedback | **Strongest mapping.** Dente et al. report pass@1 collapse — the symptom of constraint-compliance failure absent corrective feedback. The three-channel obligation report ([`oblig-0-spec.md`](oblig-0-spec.md), shipped v0.10) supplies the corrective feedback signal, converting the same failure from a pass@1 problem into a pass@k convergence problem. The settled methodological commitment in [`empirical-methodology.md`](../../experiments/methodology.md) endorses bounded-iteration measurement as a first-class empirical regime. | "Converts unbounded-divergence agent behavior under stacked constraints into bounded-iteration convergence under a structured compile-time feedback signal." |

The fourth row is the strongest *direction-of-fit* mapping the four-claim table contains, and its claim is materially different from the original "stratified verification ↔ pass@1 zeroing" framing the conversation opened with. Pass@1 is the wrong metric pairing: it is a single-attempt success rate natural for non-iterative code generation, while the three-channel obligation report is designed for iteration. Citing pass@1 as the metric the obligation report addresses would contradict [`empirical-methodology.md`'s settled disambiguation](../../experiments/methodology.md) between the design criterion (progress toward one-shot correctness) and the methodology criterion (first-round measurement is one regime among several, not the canonical one). The corrected framing — *Dente et al. measure pass@1 collapse as the symptom; LLMLL's three-channel obligation report converts the underlying constraint-compliance failure from a pass@1 problem into a pass@k convergence problem* — is empirically harder to claim (it requires showing the obligation report bounds iteration count better than free-form compile errors do), but is methodologically aligned with the project's own internal commitment and is the metric vocabulary the relevant external audiences (verification researchers, agent-evaluation researchers) natively use.

## 4. Citation discipline: three-tradition triad, not single-source

External citations should anchor Dente et al. in a triad with two adjacent-tradition references rather than citing the preprint as the sole empirical foundation. The structural argument becomes: *agent or synthesizer performance degrades monotonically as constraint complexity rises is a recurring finding across three research traditions — program synthesis under specification complexity (Solar-Lezama, ASPLOS 2006; Alur et al., FMCAD 2013), LLM instruction-following under stacked constraints (Zhou et al., 2023 — IFEval), and verified-backend agent authorship under non-functional constraint layering (Dente, Satriani, Papotti, arXiv 2605.06445, 2026). LLMLL targets the third.*

This citation discipline is load-bearing for two reasons. First, Dente et al. is a preprint that, at the time of this proposal, is approximately three weeks old; there is no peer review, no replication, and no field consensus on the instrumentation. Per the professor's literature reach (folded from the review at [`positioning-constraint-decay-review.md`](positioning-constraint-decay-review.md) §"Direct answer to Question 1"), the quadrant of verified-language agent-coding evaluations is empty — there is no Liquid Haskell, F\*, Dafny, or Idris equivalent to cross-reference. The single-source exposure on a 3-week-old preprint is real. The triad mitigates it: if Dente et al. is later refuted, withdrawn, or shown to have instrumentation flaws, the structural argument survives because Sketch/SyGuS and IFEval are both well-established and independently document the same monotonic-degradation phenomenon in adjacent domains. Second, the triad framing demonstrates to external readers — particularly the verification-and-evaluation research community — that LLMLL is reading across traditions rather than parochially within verified-PL. That is a positioning win in its own right.

## 5. Benchmark deferral as a recorded two-sided tradeoff

Running the Conduit benchmark on LLMLL is deferred indefinitely. The framing of the deferral, however, requires explicit recognition of both costs in tension rather than a one-sided design-discipline argument.

> Two costs are in tension. Running the Conduit benchmark on LLMLL would constitute benchmark-driven spec design: under benchmark pressure, the project would commit to one HTTP-framework binding among several candidates (Servant vs. raw warp vs. some `wasi.http`-plus-router hybrid), a database-and-ORM story currently absent from the spec, and a verification axis for query composition (refinement-predicates over a relational algebra? capability-typed query effects? `?proof-required` carriers for SQL?) — each a multi-quarter design effort whose outcome the project has deliberately not yet shaped, and each interacting with the v0.11 architectural-correction cluster (LT-INV, LT-CDP, LT-PPR, LT-INT) currently in flight. *Not* running the benchmark, equivalently, leaves the field's strongest currently-published empirical anchor for the failure mode unaddressed by LLMLL, creating an asymmetry external readers will notice. The project records its choice as *design-discipline over empirical-anchor-completeness*, anchored to the avoid-design-by-benchmark commitment implicit in [`language-comparison-experiments.md:27`](language-comparison-experiments.md). This is a deliberate scope position; the cost is acknowledged.

The deferral should be recorded as a new subsection in [`language-comparison-experiments.md`](language-comparison-experiments.md) titled "Deferred external benchmarks," with the two-sided framing and the citation to this proposal preserving the rationale for future archeology.

## 6. Withdrawn internal-proxy claim

The natural temptation when surfacing an external benchmark deferral is to point at an existing internal harness as a partial proxy. The first-draft framing claimed `experiments/repair-loop/` "already measures pass-rate degradation as constraints stack." That claim is wrong, and the proposal corrects to explicit withdrawal.

The repair-loop harness varies *agent × problem × target language × attempt* on a *fixed constraint set*; Dente et al. vary the *constraint stack* on a *fixed language × fixed agent × fixed contract*. The axes are perpendicular. The harness's Correctness/Assurance split is also orthogonal to L0 → L3 — Correctness/Assurance measures the *kind of evidence the same constraint set produces*, not *how constraints accumulate*. Conflating the two would not survive a careful reader, and a verification researcher reading the positioning would see the orthogonality immediately.

Corrected framing for [`language-comparison-experiments.md`](language-comparison-experiments.md):

> LLMLL has built measurement infrastructure for the language-axis variant of constraint-bearing agent authorship (`experiments/repair-loop/`, two of three problems with Python+Go testkits, TypeScript and Rust adapters pending). The constraint-layering axis Dente et al. measure is unmeasured under LLMLL and would require a sibling harness variant. The repair-loop harness's Correctness/Assurance split is orthogonal to L0→L3 — Correctness/Assurance measures kind-of-evidence under a fixed constraint set, not how constraints accumulate. The two axes can both be measured by the project, but they are different empirical regimes; conflating them in external positioning would not survive contact with a careful reader.

This is the honest framing. Admitting the perpendicularity is materially stronger than overclaiming the proxy: it demonstrates measurement discipline, and it gives experiment-lead a clean specification for the sibling harness if the deferral is ever lifted.

## 7. Vocabulary discipline for external register

Reserve "stratified verification" for project-internal docs. In external register, use "three-channel obligation report" or "tiered verification." "Stratified" is overloaded in the broader PL literature — predicative type theory, Martin-Löf universe hierarchies, stratified negation in logic programming — and will misparse for external PL readers. This is cosmetic but matters for the doc-lead promotion turn: the new subsections in [`strategic-positioning.md`](strategic-positioning.md) and [`specification-sources.md`](specification-sources.md) should adhere to the external register; the internal subsections in [`language-comparison-experiments.md`](language-comparison-experiments.md) and the new "Candidate mechanisms" table above may use either, but consistency within each consumer is the discipline.

## 9. Edge cases

Five cases enumerate the reading paths the proposal must survive after external release.

1. **External reader is a verified-language researcher (LH/F\*/Dafny/Idris author).** Under the §2 primary spec-source framing, they read the positioning and ask: *"What pathway is LLMLL operationalizing that LH+persistent+Servant cannot?"* The answer is the agent-loop integration around an existing verification surface (§3 framing) — not a claim about the verification machinery itself, which the design-reference set has had for years. **Channel: positioning — the reader's question is answered in the secondary-mechanisms subsection by design.** **Citation:** §3, third paragraph.
2. **External reader is an LLM-evaluation researcher.** They ask for LLMLL-on-Conduit numbers. The answer under §5's two-sided-tradeoff framing is: deliberate deferral, recorded as scope choice, with the reasoning in the public design folder. **Channel: positioning — the answer is pre-formulated and citable.** **Citation:** §5; [`language-comparison-experiments.md:27`](language-comparison-experiments.md).
3. **External reader is from the IFEval / SyGuS research community.** They read the §4 triad-citation framing and recognize the structural argument is built on their tradition. **Channel: positioning win.** **Citation:** §4.
4. **The paper is later refuted, withdrawn, or shown to have instrumentation flaws.** The §4 triad-citation framing means the structural argument survives — it is anchored in Sketch/SyGuS and IFEval as well, both well-established. The Dente et al. citation becomes "one preprint cited at the time of writing, since refuted; the underlying constraint-decay phenomenon is also documented in Sketch/SyGuS and IFEval traditions." **Channel: structural resilience built into citation discipline.** **Citation:** §4.
5. **[`specification-sources.md §1`](specification-sources.md) itself becomes the next contested doc.** If the spec-from-external-standards bet is the primary positioning move and the source doc is more than a year old, the source doc itself becomes a critique target. The April 2026 byline is approximately six weeks old as of this writing, so immediate exposure is low. The doc should be reviewed at the next strategic-positioning pass for drift against any newer external work. **Channel: spec-drift (potential, deferred).** **Citation:** `specification-sources.md` byline.
